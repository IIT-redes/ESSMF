Flexible access under constrained network capacity

Flexible connection agreement

A flexible connection turns scarce network capacity into explicit, reviewable conditions for access, curtailment and responsibility.

Use the four pillars to describe why flexible access is offered, what the contract contains, how curtailment operates, and how risks and value are allocated.

Explore the four pillarsHow to review

Overview and orientation

Flexible access is a connection right with conditions

A flexible connection allows electrical capacity to connect while injection or withdrawal can be limited or controlled under agreed conditions. The arrangement therefore combines a firm part of the connection with clearly defined flexible access conditions, so the user can understand what capacity is guaranteed and what capacity may be restricted.

Key language for reading the framework

Flexible connection agreement

Agreed connection conditions that allow network access while permitting limits or control of electricity injection and/or withdrawal.

Firm capacity

The portion of injection or withdrawal capacity that is treated as firm and not subject to the flexible restriction.

Flexible capacity

Additional or alternative capacity that can be connected subject to agreed time, capacity or operating limits.

Curtailment

A temporary reduction or restriction of the user’s permitted injection or withdrawal under the agreed flexible conditions.

Curtailment notification

The notice horizon between informing the user and the curtailment event; it may range from day-ahead to real-time or ex-post for unforeseen events.

Curtailment allocation

The rule that decides how constrained capacity is shared among flexible users, for example pro-rata, LIFO, auction or congestion contribution.

Connection cost

The cost allocated for connecting the user and, where applicable, reinforcing the network for the requested capacity.

Network charge

The ongoing tariff treatment applied to firm and/or flexible use of network capacity.

Balance responsibility

The assignment of financial responsibility for imbalances when curtailment changes scheduled energy.

Value stacking

Use of the same flexible asset or affected energy in compatible commercial arrangements in addition to the connection agreement.

What is the proposal?

Describe every flexible connection through the same four design pillars

The framework converts a broad “non-firm connection” concept into explicit design choices that can be compared across countries and projects.

Headline question

Can a flexible connection be described clearly enough that another expert can understand the rationale, contract, operation and economic consequences without relying on undocumented local practice?

Why does it matter?

Faster connection is valuable only when the conditions are transparent and workable

Flexible connections can accelerate grid access, make better use of scarce hosting capacity, and avoid or defer reinforcement when that is efficient. Their value depends on transparent curtailment rules, predictable limits, a clear pathway to firm access where relevant, and coordination with tariffs and flexibility markets so that risks and incentives are not shifted unfairly.

Who is affected?

The agreement links system operation, regulation and the connected user

System operators, regulators or competent authorities, connected users, balance-responsible parties, aggregators and market actors may all be affected by the allocation of access and curtailment responsibility.

Where is it applicable?

Areas with limited or unavailable network capacity

Flexible connections are most relevant where requested firm capacity cannot be offered immediately because network capacity is limited or unavailable. They may be temporary while reinforcement is delivered, or remain permanent where network development is not the most efficient solution. Depending on the framework, generation, demand and storage can all be eligible.
