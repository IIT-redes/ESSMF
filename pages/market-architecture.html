Market architecture

Source-grounded tutorial

How to think about this pillar

Describes the market as interacting sub-markets: what exists, when it operates, what it trades, where it applies, and who participates.

Market-design intelligence

Read the pillar as a design procedure

Start here: these choices define the market objects that the other pillars coordinate, clear, operate, and constrain.

1. Define each sub-market

List the sub-markets and record GOT, GCT, MTU and market type.

  • Use one consistent market label across all five pillar tables.
  • Keep timing values explicit enough to compare interacting markets.

2. Define service and product

Separate the system service from the tradable product used to procure it.

  • Record product type and technical requirements.
  • Do not use service and product as synonyms.

3. Define location and roles

Record spatial granularity, responsible SO, voltage level, buyer, seller and market operator.

  • State eligible technologies and aggregation rules.
  • Record whether participation is optional, compulsory or hybrid.

4. Check interoperability

Use timing, location, product and actor choices to identify barriers to participation or coordination.

  • Look for timing or granularity mismatches.
  • Check whether roles remain clear when resources join several markets.

Expert feedback

Evaluate this pillar

Can this pillar describe an existing flexibility market unambiguously and guide a new distribution-level market?

  • Are sub-market number, GOT, GCT, MTU and market type sufficient?
  • Are service, product and technical requirements clearly separated?
  • Are location, actor, eligibility and aggregation fields sufficient?
  • What is missing, ambiguous or too context-specific for cross-country comparison?
